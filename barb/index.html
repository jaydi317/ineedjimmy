Part Two · The number

You said it without flinching: $50,000. Roughly ten years unfiled.

Now here's what nobody has told you about that number.

$50,000 is a fear number, not an assessment.

It's a figure your mind built in the dark, out of a decade of not looking. And there are four specific reasons the real number is very likely to be materially smaller:

1
Ten years of business expenses you never claimed. You're a commissioned real estate agent — self-employed. Vehicle, phone, marketing, desk fees, home office, licensing, insurance, professional dues. Ten years of legitimate deductions that have never been applied to a single one of those years. Tax is owed on net income, not gross. This alone can change the number dramatically.
2
Ten years of credits you're 81 years old and entitled to. The age amount, the basic personal amount, the pension income amount, medical expense credits — and you've had significant medical years since 72. None of it applied. All of it available.
3
Penalties and interest can be cancelled — there's a form for it. Form RC4288, Taxpayer Relief. CRA can waive penalties and interest for serious illness, financial hardship, and extraordinary circumstances. You are 81, you had a heart incident at 72, you live with stenosis, scoliosis and constant pain, and money is tight. On paper you are close to a textbook candidate. Most people never apply because nobody tells them the form exists. A large share of what feels like "the $50,000" in cases like yours is penalty and interest — the part that is most negotiable.
4
Benefits may flow back toward you. In Canada, GIS, the GST/HST credit and the Ontario Trillium Benefit are all triggered by filing. Ten years of not filing may mean ten years of not receiving. Some of that is recoverable retroactively when the returns go in. For some seniors, back-filing has ended in a cheque.

None of that is a promise. It's an honest accounting of why the number in the drawer and the number in your head are two different numbers — and why you've been carrying the heavier one for a decade for no reason.

The one genuinely urgent thing

If CRA files for you before you file for yourself, they can issue what's called a notional assessment — they estimate your income and allow zero expenses. Every deduction in item 1 above disappears.

That is the actual difference between going first and being caught. It is not about morality or getting in trouble. It is about whether you get to claim ten years of expenses or none. Your son has been telling you to go first. He is right, and now you know exactly why.

There's also the Voluntary Disclosures Program (VDP) — come forward before CRA contacts you and you can get relief from penalties plus partial interest relief. Come forward after, and you can't. That door is open right now. It closes the day a letter arrives.
