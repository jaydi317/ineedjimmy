## PHASE 0 — THE TAXES

**This is the first domino. Do this one first.**

### The facts

- I have not filed a Canadian personal tax return in **approximately ten years.**
- The number I'm afraid of is **$50,000.** I do not know the real number.
- I am **self-employed on commission** as a real estate agent.
- I own a **townhouse** with equity. I've considered a mortgage or reverse mortgage and I have fears about it.
- **I do have some money.**
- My son is a financial advisor and keeps telling me: *do it before they come to you.*
- I called a friend's accountant. I was supposed to go find out my last filed year. I haven't.
- I've told almost nobody. When asked what I'm most afraid people would think, the words were **shame** and **fear** — and specifically, fear that even after the taxes are paid I won't be able to support myself.

### Why $50,000 is probably not the real number

Four reasons, and every one of them is worth money:

**1. Ten years of unclaimed business expenses.** I'm self-employed on commission. Vehicle, phone, marketing, desk fees, home office, licensing, insurance, professional dues. Tax is owed on *net* income, not gross. None of this has ever been applied.

**2. Ten years of unclaimed personal credits.** The age amount (65+), the basic personal amount, medical expense credits — and I've had significant medical years since 72.

**3. Penalties and interest can be cancelled.** *Form RC4288 — Taxpayer Relief.* CRA can waive penalties and interest for **serious illness, financial hardship, and extraordinary circumstances.** There's a ten-year window. I am 81, had a heart incident at 72, live with stenosis, scoliosis and constant pain, and money is tight. Most people never apply because nobody tells them the form exists.

**4. Benefits may flow back toward me.** In Canada, GIS, the GST/HST credit and the Ontario Trillium Benefit are triggered by *filing.* Ten years of not filing may mean ten years of not receiving. Some of that is recoverable retroactively.

### ⚠️ THE ONE GENUINELY URGENT THING

If CRA files *for* me before I file for myself, they can issue a **notional assessment** — they estimate my income and allow **zero expenses.** Every deduction in reason #1 above disappears.

That's the real reason to go first. Not morality. Not getting in trouble. **It's the difference between claiming ten years of expenses and claiming none.**

There is also the **Voluntary Disclosures Program (VDP)**: come forward *before* CRA contacts you and you can get penalty relief plus partial interest relief. Come forward after, and you can't. That door is open right now.

### THE FIRST STEP — 20 MINUTES, WITHOUT LEAVING THE HOUSE

Call **CRA individual enquiries: 1-800-959-8281**

Say: *"I'd like to confirm the status of my account."* Then write down these three answers:

1. **"What is the last tax year that has been assessed for me?"**
2. **"What is my current balance owing, if any?"**
3. **"Is there any collection action or legal action on my account?"**

I am not filing anything. I am not admitting anything. I'm asking a clerk to read three numbers off a screen.

### THE SECOND STEP — WITHIN TWO WEEKS

Call three accountants. Before anything else, ask: **"Have you filed CRA Voluntary Disclosures applications before?"** Hire the one who says yes without hesitating.
