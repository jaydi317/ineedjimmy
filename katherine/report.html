PART II — THE INCLUSIONS

The Flaws in the Stone (You'd Fire Us for Flattery)

Inclusion #1 — Founder gravity

You are 77, your name is on the door, and the Council suspects most of that beautiful referral flow routes through you personally. Buyers don't discount firms for having a great founder; they discount firms that can't prove the machine runs without her — and they take that discount by moving your money from "wired at closing" to "earned over four years, if." Every month of documented de-risking moves dollars back across that line. This is the single most expensive flaw, and the most fixable.

Inclusion #2 — The invisible empire

Your homepage shows no client count, no assets, no referral rate, no books, no awards, no story. Meanwhile the strongest competitor in your niche prints "20,000 clients and $3.9B" on their front door. A doctor comparing you side-by-side concludes they're twenty times more trusted. They are not. But in marketing — and in diligence — hidden proof is priced identically to absent proof: zero.

Inclusion #3 — A landmine in your own language

You describe the firm as "no commissions." Your own website FAQ discloses insurance-carrier compensation, and your SEC filing lists eleven licensed insurance agents. Both facts can live together honestly — "our advisors earn no commissions" is true and powerful — but the blanket version will be read, aloud, by a buyer's lawyer at the worst possible moment. Fix the words before anyone with a checkbook reads them.

Inclusion #4 — The numbers disagree with each other

"Over 500 physicians" on one page, "over 600" on another, 650 in conversation; four advisors here, six there, eleven advisory staff on the filing. Trivia today. In diligence, inconsistency is a tax — buyers discount whatever they can't reconcile. One afternoon of cleanup, permanent return.

You Called the Top. The Data Agrees.

You told Jimmy you sense the market peaking. Here's what the record shows — and it's better than your instinct suggested.

2025 was the most active year in history for selling firms like yours: 322 transactions, an 18% jump over the previous record, with the median firm changing hands at a record 11.6× adjusted profit — up roughly 40% from just five years ago. You don't have to choose between selling and working. The market pre-built your preference.

What's it worth? The honest answer — and beware anyone who gives you a different one — is that the price is a formula: your adjusted profit × a multiple set by the flaws and facets above. Firms in your size band traded around 7.5–10× adjusted profit in 2025; your five facets argue for the top of that band or through it, your two structural inclusions argue for the bottom. That spread is worth millions, and here is the entire thesis of this report: the spread is not decided by the market. It is decided by the 18 months before you go to market. The cut plan is Part V.
