Why kids progress

Most gyms put 12 kids in a line and one coach at the front.

At AirBound, every class splits into small groups of 4 or 5 with their own coach. The groups rotate through three stations every class:

01

where kids learn to fly safely

02

The spring floor

where they put it together into a real tumbling pass

03

The skill circuit

the drills that turn "almost" into "I did it"

Three stations. One coach for every 4–5 kids. That's why moms tell us their kid learned more in one class than in months somewhere else.

Here's how it works

  1. Pick a class time. Tap the button, pick the day that works. Takes about a minute.
  2. Bring her in comfy clothes, hair pulled back, no jewelry. We handle the rest.
  3. She joins a group of 4 or 5 kids with a coach. Same class the enrolled kids take — not a watered-down demo.
  4. After class, her coach tells you exactly how she did and which class she belongs in. Then you decide. No pressure. Really.

Why the first class is free

Because a mom can't tell from a website whether her kid will love it. She can tell in ten minutes on the floor.

We'd rather you see it than read about it.

Questions moms ask

Can we start now, or do we wait for a new session?

Now. We enroll any week of the year.

What if she's never done anything like this?

Most kids haven't. First-timers are usually 5–7 and start in Beginner Tumbling.

What if she's shy?

Small groups are exactly why shy kids do well here. Her coach will know her name by the end of the first class.

Is it safe?

Spring floors, a foam pit, coaches who spot every skill, and a 5-to-1 ratio. Safety is the whole reason the groups are small.

A note from Jenna

Every kid needs
one place that's theirs.

I didn't grow up tumbling. I was a swimmer. I'm a nurse by training, a mom of four, and for years I ran a business helping people take care of their families.

Two and a half years ago I saw a post from a family in Rexburg. A woman named ValaRae had built this gym from nothing, run it for 17 years, and loved it right up until she passed away. Her family had kept it going for a year but couldn't anymore. They were about to sell the equipment and close the doors. [verify details with Jenna]

I called that afternoon.

I didn't buy a gym because I know tumbling. I bought it because I know what a kid looks like when she finds the one place she feels brave.

Maybe school is hard right now. Maybe home is. Maybe she's just the quiet one and hasn't found her thing. For an hour a week, none of that comes in the door with her. She gets a coach who knows her name, a group of four other kids, and a skill she couldn't do last week.

That's what we're building here. It happens to be tumbling. It could have been anything.

The coaches who taught here before I came are still here. The floor is one of the best in Rexburg. We remodeled, we kept the foam pit, and we kept the rule ValaRae set: never more than 5 kids per coach.

Come see it. The first class is on us.

— Jenna Henrie, Owner
