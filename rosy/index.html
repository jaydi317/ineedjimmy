Jimmy’s first take

The smartest shape for three days

Keep each day in one part of London. That means more London and less time underground. This is the opening sketch; your answers decide the final version.

Day one 01

Royal London, right outside your door

Start gently after travel and let the landmarks come to you. Your hotel area makes this the easiest win.

  • Westminster Bridge, Big Ben & the Abbey
  • St James’s Park to Buckingham Palace
  • Covent Garden or a West End show
  • London Eye near sunset
Day two 02

Tower, market & the Thames

Book the Tower at opening, then reward yourselves with lunch at Borough Market. Walk the river only as far as feels good.

  • Tower of London at opening
  • Tower Bridge photos
  • Borough Market lunch
  • Tate Modern, St Paul’s view & South Bank
Day three 03

The London that feels like Rosy

This is the flex day: antiques, flowers, fashion, museums, neighborhoods, or a long lunch. We’ll choose after you answer.

  • Portobello if it’s Saturday
  • Columbia Road if it’s Sunday
  • Notting Hill, Kensington or Camden
  • One beautiful final dinner
Best first restaurant pick near the London Eye

Skylon at the Royal Festival Hall.

It’s close enough to stroll home, but it still feels like an occasion: Modern British food, a dramatic room, and floor-to-ceiling views of the Thames. Ask for a river-facing table and time dinner around sunset.

The market shortlist

Choose by mood, not hype

Borough is the sure thing for food. After that, the best market depends on which three dates are the London dates and what you actually love hunting for.

Best first market · Food

Borough Market

The essential London food market. Go near opening—especially on Saturday—then pair it with the Tower, Tower Bridge, or a Thames walk.

Hours & official guide ↗
Best Saturday · Antiques & vintage

Portobello Road

Colorful Notting Hill streets, antiques, vintage finds, people-watching, and a proper browse. Saturday has the fullest market energy.

Explore Portobello ↗
Best Sunday · Flowers & East London

Columbia Road

A flower-packed Sunday morning with independent shops. It combines naturally with Brick Lane and Spitalfields afterward.
