Boring Bag Lady — The 90-Day Restart

make money again, spend nothing, keep it simple

This plan is built on one big idea: your website doesn't make money — your system does. Etsy gave you visitors and you still couldn't make it work, so the fix isn't "more traffic." The fix is selling to people who already love your bags, capturing everyone you meet, and letting custom orders do the heavy lifting. Everything below is free.

The 3 things that make the money (in order):
  1. Custom orders — paid up front, no leftover inventory, your highest profit. This is the engine.
  2. Your list — every person who buys or shows interest goes on an email/text list you own forever.
  3. Free, warm traffic — in-person markets + Facebook. The people who touch your bags buy your bags.
Why no paid ads, ever: ads spend money to reach cold strangers who don't trust you yet. You already have something better and free — markets where people hold your bags, and Facebook where they already know you. We pour gas on what's already working.

Days 1–30 · Foundation

Goal: be able to take money online, and make your first online sale.

Week 1 — Get the basics live

  • Pick which mockup you like (A or B). Jimmy makes it live on boringbaglady.com.
  • Open a free Stripe or PayPal account so you can get paid.
  • Photograph 6–10 finished bags by a window. Natural light, plain background.

Week 2 — Turn the site into a store

  • Make a "payment link" for each bag and drop it into the site (Jimmy helps).
  • Re-open your Facebook page. Pin a post: "I'm back — handmade one-of-a-kind bags."
  • Start a Google Sheet called "My Customers." Add every past buyer you can remember.

Week 3–4 — Start selling where buyers already are

  • List 3–5 bags on Facebook Marketplace (free, local, people ready to buy).
  • Post one bag a day on your Facebook page with the price and the website link.
  • At your next in-person market, collect names + cell numbers ("text JOIN to hear about new bags" or a simple sign-up sheet by a "win a free bag" jar).
The capture habit: every single person who buys or even lingers at your table goes on the list. A buyer you can reach again is worth ten strangers. This one habit is the difference between starting over every month and building something.
Days 31–60 · Rhythm

Goal: predictable weekly sales + your first custom order.

The weekly rhythm (same thing every week)

  • Monday: photograph the new bags you finished.
  • Tuesday: "New Bag Tuesday" — post them on Facebook + email your list a quick note.
  • Friday: list 2–3 bags on Facebook Marketplace.

Launch the custom-order offer

  • Post: "I'm opening 6 custom bag slots this month — design your own one-of-a-kind bag with me." Point them to the form on your site.
  • When a request comes in, reply within a day with a price. Take payment up front before you start.
  • Share photos of the custom bag as you make it — people love watching it come to life (and it sells the next one).

Start Pinterest (your free long-term traffic)

  • Make a free Pinterest business account. Pin every bag photo with a title + your website link.
  • Pin 3–5 a week. Pinterest keeps sending you free visitors for years — it's slow but it compounds.
  • Ask your happy buyers for a one-line review + a photo. Post these everywhere.
Days 61–90 · Amplify

Goal: a repeatable system running without you reinventing it — custom orders as steady income.

Turn one customer into three

  • Referral offer: "Send a friend who buys, and your next custom bag is $25 off." Tell every buyer.
  • Email your list a "design your own for the holidays / a gift" custom push.
  • Ask 2–3 happiest customers if they'll show their bag in a quick photo or video. Real people sell bags.

Add one steady local outlet

  • Get into one recurring spot — a regular market booth, or a local boutique/salon that sells a few of your bags on consignment.
  • Keep the weekly rhythm going. Don't add new channels — go deeper on the 2–3 that work.
By Day 90 you should have:
  • A live website that takes payments — for free.
  • A growing list of customers you own and can sell to again.
  • A weekly posting rhythm that brings steady sales.
  • Custom orders coming in — paid up front, your best profit.
  • Pinterest quietly sending free visitors every week.
The one rule if you forget everything else: never let a person who liked your bag walk away without getting on your list. Capture, then sell to them again and again. That's the whole game.
