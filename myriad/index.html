Market research · what the winning schools actually do

We funnel-hacked the best schools in the world. Seven things repeat.

£29 > Free

The best schools charge a small fee for the trial (2 weeks + a FREE uniform) — and convert far better than "first class free." Every club in Barnet gives it away free. Nobody runs the paid trial. That's an open lane.

Parents buy confidence

Not taekwondo. The winning headlines sell focus, confidence and bully-proofing. The trigger moments: a bullying incident, school behaviour worries, back-to-school (September — right now) and New Year.

50–60% vs 20–30%

Schools that script the trial child's first visit — greeted by name, uniform moment, photo, warm parent chat at the end — convert 50–60% of trials. Unscripted drop-ins convert 20–30%. You ARE this system, Priya.

  1. The paid trial with a keep-the-uniform guarantee. "£29 for 2 weeks + free uniform. Not delighted? Full refund — and your child keeps the uniform." It filters the tyre-kickers and makes the child feel they already belong before the first kick.
  2. Speed and follow-up. Answering an enquiry within minutes multiplies sign-ups; the best schools follow up 5+ times over two weeks. Most clubs try once and give up.
  3. Gradings every 12 weeks, made ceremonial. Certificates, photos, applause. It's the retention engine, the revenue rhythm, AND the referral moment ("bring a friend to watch grading day").
  4. Family stacking. Siblings and parents training together — one trip, two or three memberships. GKR Karate built an empire on this. A family of black belts teaching families is YOUR unfair advantage.
  5. Referrals as a system, not an accident. Double-sided rewards (both families get a free month), buddy passes handed out at belt ceremonies. At good schools, 1 in 4 new students comes this way.
  6. One-to-one lessons priced as premium. Private tuition with a senior Dan grade sells for £30–£60 a session. Your quiet Saturday is currently giving this away for free.
  7. The ladder. Trial → membership → Black Belt journey → leadership track. Every winner walks families up a ladder instead of selling one class at a time.
