A room worth remembering.

Eleven visual references, three local rivals, and three distinct directions. Independent research first; Claude’s swipe file second.

The five biggest insights

The room is the identity.

The orange and yellow chairs, blue wall, hand-painted Indy mural, and long counter are more distinctive than a commodity vape montage. Treat them as the signature asset.

Premium comes from editing.

Crop decisively, give the type room, and choose one visual idea. Davidoff and La Cabra show how coherence changes the perceived value of a familiar category.

Hospitality is the useful adjacent category.

Death & Co makes the room the invitation. Just Vapor’s supplied Cheers review supports the same approach using its actual bar, with no invented lifestyle scene.

Make vape-first visible.

Higher Grade and Indy E Cigs explicitly combine smoke and vape. Mason’s supplied words provide a sharper proposition; the design should keep glass and cannabis branding out of the focal areas.

Local convenience needs a local interface.

Call, reserve, pick up, show ID. Ecommerce carts, shipping language, and a simulated checkout would create the wrong expectation. The phone is the primary canvas.

The market, through a designer’s eyes

These are design judgments, not conversion, traffic or ranking claims. Independent notes were saved before opening Claude’s swipe file. Inspected public pages in a desktop browser on October 6. US geolocation produced US storefront redirects for Davidoff and La Cabra. The research covers the named sources below; it is a selected reference set, not an exhaustive market census.

Large pale campaign stages, sharply lit hardware, assertive type, a black nicotine-warning band and a compact white header.

Control of light, product scale, and foreground/background separation.

Tonal control and photographic art direction, rather than a pile of unrelated catalog images.

The carousel and big retail taxonomy slow the path to a nearby store. Gold alone is an unhelpful shortcut for Just Vapor.

After reading Claude’s swipe file

Where I agree

The bar is the best human invitation; the mural is the strongest ownable graphic; Mason’s “vape shop first” statement should organize the experience. First-visit explanations and customer language are more useful here than catalog theatrics. The correct phone number, visible pickup flow, and yellow owner questions should survive every visual direction.

Where I disagree

“Not one competitor shows a single review on their homepage” is too broad. Higher Grade and Indy E Cigs both expose customer reviews on their current homepage. The advantage is Just Vapor’s supplied review language about belonging and attention, not the mere existence of testimonials. Online discount patterns also do not prove that a particular local offer converts.

The claim that nobody shows a store or people is too broad as well: Higher Grade uses a staffed store hero, and Indy Vapor Shop has an interior photograph. Just Vapor’s room is unusually specific; that is the useful distinction.

What was missing

Hospitality and international specialty retail offer richer design references than vape-commerce catalogs alone. Photo editing needs special attention: the supplied wide frames include cannabis signage and general glass inventory. The concepts use tighter real crops to honor the vape-first brief. The bow-tie V is visible in the mural, but no clean logo file is supplied; the headers retain Claude’s supplied bow-tie V stand-in alongside the name, and the real mark stays in the mural photograph.

Reservation forms in the supplied pages simulate a successful text. The new concepts instead disclose their preview state and never claim a request was sent. The actual reservation, POS, rewards and SMS connections still need confirmation. I have not revalidated Claude’s legal, advertising, hemp-date or directory-rating claims; they are not used as new facts in this design research.

Brief check

The directions change composition, typography, color balance and photographic treatment rather than recoloring one template. Good Company is an architectural hospitality composition; Cloud / City is a graphic poster; The Counter is a light retail interface. I rejected a black-and-neon vape treatment and a beige serif boutique treatment because neither comes from this room. Orange, blue and yellow have specific, photographed reasons to exist here.

Source and build boundaries

The supplied ZIP contains four real store photographs, three Claude homepages, a picker and a swipe file. Those five HTML originals are preserved byte for byte. The new pages are standalone HTML with embedded photo crops; Google Fonts are the only optional external design asset. Core content, age gate, and reservation preview work without those fonts.

Ratings, review text, hours, offers, Castleton details and rewards come from Claude’s source pages. Unconfirmed facts remain yellow. No delta-8 or THC product is featured. No shipping, checkout, payment collection or actual reservation submission is implemented in these concepts.

Design briefs saved before homepage construction. Visual source screenshots and independent notes are retained locally; third-party photography is not used in the concepts.
