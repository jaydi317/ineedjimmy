# Bruce the Piano Man: scroll-worlds concept sheet

Written before the build, Oct 6, 2026.

**The rule:** one drawn object that physically changes as you scroll and carries the whole story. The scroll is one night at Bruce's piano: from the opener to last call. A photo swapping behind text fails.

**The thread:** Bruce plays the room, not the piano. Every gag has to pass the brief's "night test": did this really happen in the room? Real Bruce (photos) is the proof; the drawn world is the frame. The two doors, "Get a Bruce Alert" and "Book Bruce," sit on every screen.

## A: "88 Keys" (the cinematic wow)
**Object:** a glossy black-and-white keyboard seen from just above the keys, running away from you into the night like a runway. Midnight sky, string lights, neon.
**What scroll does:** you fly down the keys. A readout counts KEY 01/88 to 88/88. Eight beats of the night, eleven keys each: the opener (Hail Purdue, title only), the first hour with no tips, the sticky-note requests (the notes land and stay on the keys), the groan ("If I Had a Million Dollars," again), "Say social!", the flashback (the runway goes sepia and the counter reads 7 h, 1 h, 15 min), the band (the runway splits into eight lit lanes: same Bruce, bigger party), and the room (real photos, "since 1995"). Last call: the key cover slides shut over all 88 lit keys, one gold light stays on, and it becomes the two doors.
**Feeling:** "a whole night with this guy goes by in one breath."
**Type:** Tilt Neon (tube lettering) + Yellowtail (sign painter) + Inter Tight + JetBrains Mono for the readout. Midnight #080C22, neon pink, neon green, warm gold.

## B: "The Request Jukebox" (the fun one)
**Object:** a fat, chrome-and-candy cartoon jukebox with an arched neon top, bubble tubes, a record window and a strip of song-title tabs.
**What scroll does:** it starts unplugged. Each beat of the night lights one more arch segment, swings a new record onto the platter (titles only), fills the bubble tubes and sticks another request on the glass. Cartoon Bruce (Jimmy's existing character art) stands next to it and changes pose: pointing for the opener, the tip bucket, the groan, "Say social!", the wink, the wig. Real photos pop up as Polaroids: the proof that it's a real guy. At last call the jukebox unlocks and it's yours to play: tap a title and Bruce reacts. Type "IU" on a sticky note and he refuses it. The jukebox's A/B selector then becomes "What kind of night?" for booking.
**Feeling:** "I want to be in that room."
**Type:** Shrikhand (chunky retro script) + Permanent Marker (sticky notes) + DM Sans. Hawaiian blue, sticky pink, request yellow on a warm plum night.

## C: "Two Sides" (premium, bookers first)
**Object:** a large, quiet, beautifully drawn generic cassette in cream and ink, no brand, no year.
**What scroll does:** the reels turn and the tape physically moves from the left spool to the right. The counter is the story: 7:00:00, 1:00:00, 0:15:00 (how he taught himself), then it rolls into years: 1995 (the first night), 2026 (the band, the boat). Side A is **The Piano Bar**: who books it and what's in it. The cassette flips over in 3D to Side B, **The Full Band**: eight pieces, stage, sound and lights. The case insert unfolds; its track list is the real 2026 show archive. The last track is "A Pirate Looks at Forty." The tape runs out at 2027, and the blank label becomes the booking form.
**Feeling:** for a reunion chair or an alumni club: "this is easy, and he's the real thing."
**Type:** Instrument Serif + Inter Tight + Space Mono (label-maker). Cassette cream, ink, warm gold, one pink stripe.

## Why they're genuinely different
- A = a **camera move** (you travel through the night). Dark, neon, cinematic.
- B = a **toy** (you play with the night). Warm, loud, cartoon.
- C = an **artifact** (you hold the night in your hand). Quiet, cream, editorial; built for the person signing the check.

## My pick
**A for the homepage, with B's jukebox as the toy section and C's form styling for /book.** A is the only one where the scroll itself *is* the product: a Bruce night, start to finish, in 30 seconds. It also says "88 keys, one night" in a way no dueling-piano rival can copy. If Bruce reacts to the cartoon as "kids' TV," C is the safe, premium fallback.
