How a Match Happens

A single call. A short intake. The right person, ready.

Districts rarely have time for a search when the situation is urgent. Our process is deliberately simple, so the district can keep leading while we prepare the person.

01 · Intake

You tell us what happened.

A short, confidential conversation with a former Indiana superintendent — not a sales representative. We listen for the shape of the problem, the calendar, and the sensitivities.

02 · Match

We choose from the bench.

We select from 50+ experienced Indiana leaders based on the specific role, district size, community context, and calendar. Most placements begin within days, not weeks.

03 · Steady the Building

Someone experienced is on‑site.

Whether it's a next‑day interim principal, a superintendent search, or a finance review — the district keeps moving. Our person carries the calendar so your board can lead.

When Districts Usually Call

You do not have to solve this alone.

Every district hits terrain it has never crossed before. Below are the moments an experienced call to Administrator Assistance changes the week.

Sudden Vacancy

A principal or central‑office leader has just left.

You need someone stabilizing the building while you plan the permanent role — not a résumé pile.

Superintendent Search

The board is preparing to hire the district's next leader.

You want a search led by people who have sat in the chair — with a shortlist your board can defend publicly.

Budget & Finance

A referendum, audit, or budget cliff is approaching.

You need experienced eyes on the numbers, the assumptions, and the community conversation.

Bargaining Season

Collective bargaining is around the corner.

You want a chief negotiator who has done this in Indiana districts, at the table and in the community.

Strategic Planning

The board is ready for a plan the community will trust.

Facilitated by people who have run districts — not consultants speaking a language school boards do not use.

Operational Assessment

Transportation, technology, or curriculum needs an honest review.

A quiet outside audit by former practitioners — reported to the superintendent first, then to the board.
