The only rule: specific stories beat business language.

If a question reminds you of a real phone call, district, problem, or moment, tell us that story. Please leave out confidential student or personnel information.

1

What is the business, really?

Help us see the company through the eyes of the person who calls you.

Pretend I know nothing. Who calls Administrator Assistance, what has usually just happened when they call, and what do you actually do for them?

2

Show us the transformation.

A true story gives us the clearest proof and the best language.

Tell us about one engagement you are especially proud of. What was happening before they called, what did your team do, and what was different afterward?

3

Why do they choose you?

We want the explanation that works in a real conversation.

Why do districts choose you instead of handling it internally, hiring one person, or using another firm? What do you say on the phone that makes people immediately understand the value?

4

Where does the business come from?

This tells us whether the opportunity is marketing, follow-up, packaging, or something else.

Where did your last ten good projects come from? Which two or three services create most of the business—and where do good opportunities fall through the cracks?

5

What is frustrating?

This is often where the most useful system is hiding.

What is not working as well as it should? What do you hate doing, procrastinate on, or wish would disappear from your week? What still has to pass through you personally that should not?

6

Imagine the blank-check future.

Before we decide what is practical, we want to understand what “better” means to you.

If you had a blank check and five excellent people for a year, what would you change first? What would the company—and your own life—look like twelve months later?

7

What would make this worthwhile?

Give the Council a finish line instead of making us guess.

What could the Council put in front of you that would make you say, “That’s exactly what I’ve been trying to explain” or “I can actually use this tomorrow”? What do outsiders usually misunderstand?
