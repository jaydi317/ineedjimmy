Table 1 · Families & estates

The kitchen table, after the funeral.

Three people, three ideas about what Mom wanted, and a probate court waiting. We guide personal representatives and heirs through estate administration, and represent families in disputes over wills, estates and guardianships.

Table 2 · Business owners

The table where the partnership comes apart.

Operating agreements, buyouts, contracts, succession, and the disputes when a deal goes sideways: breach of contract, collections, fraud and breach-of-duty claims.

Gene Wheeler spent 7 years litigating creditors’ rights, then 14 years in-house at a Fortune 1000 commercial lender and one of the largest private companies in the country.

Table 3 · Mediation

The table where two sides meet in the middle.

Mediation and arbitration across civil matters, from family disputes to bankruptcy. Often faster and cheaper than a trial, and the parties keep control of the outcome.

James Ammeen is on the Indiana registry as a civil mediator and a domestic relations mediator.

Table 4 · Litigation

And when it can’t be settled: the counsel table.

Commercial litigation in state and federal court. James is admitted in Indiana, the Seventh Circuit, the Southern and Northern Districts of Indiana, and the Northern District of Illinois.

He spent two years at the Indiana Supreme Court as law clerk to Chief Justice Randall T. Shepard (1995–97).

Table 5 · Credit unions

The seat at the end of the board table.

Outside general counsel for credit unions: regulatory compliance, contracts and vendors, HR questions, litigation and strategy, without the cost of a full-time legal department.

Gene has been the client: 14 years in-house, hiring and supervising outside litigation counsel. Proud sponsor of America’s Credit Unions.

How do I know if mediation is right for us?

If both sides are still willing to talk, mediation is often worth trying before a lawsuit. If they aren’t, we’ll tell you that too, and what litigation would involve.

Can you handle an estate if the family already disagrees?

Yes. We represent personal representatives and heirs in probate administration and in probate disputes over wills, estates and guardianships.

We’re an out-of-state firm. Can you act as local counsel?

Yes, in Indiana state and federal courts, including for firms from counties outside Central Indiana.
