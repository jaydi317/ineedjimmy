Where it hurts

Elbow pain

From the golf course to the keyboard, elbows take a beating. Find out what's really going on.

Book My First Visit

Why see an extremity specialist

Dr. McCray is a Certified Chiropractic Extremity Practitioner. That means added training in the joints beyond the spine, including the elbow, wrist and shoulder. Elbows are part of his everyday work.

Common reasons elbows hurt

  • Outside of the elbow ("tennis elbow"): often from gripping, lifting, racquet sports, pickleball or using a mouse all day.
  • Inside of the elbow ("golfer's elbow"): often from golf, throwing, climbing or lifting.
  • Throwing and overhead sports: baseball, softball, volleyball.
  • Pain that starts somewhere else: the neck, shoulder or wrist can send pain or tingling into the elbow.

How we figure out yours

Your exam checks the elbow itself, how you grip and move, and the neck, shoulder and wrist, because the sore spot isn't always the source. X-rays are taken only if there's a clinical reason.

What care can include

  • Adjustments of the elbow, wrist or shoulder when the exam calls for it
  • Myofascial release, cupping or dry needling for tight forearm muscles
  • Exercise rehab to strengthen the forearm and fix the movement behind it

When we refer you out

If the exam points to something that needs imaging, a specialist or a surgeon, such as a suspected fracture, a torn tendon or a nerve problem, Dr. McCray tells you and refers you to the right provider.

Go to urgent care or the ER if

  • Your elbow looks deformed or you can't move it after a fall or hit
  • You heard a pop with sudden weakness or a bulge in the upper arm
  • It's hot, red and swollen, especially with a fever

Elbow keeping you off the court or the keyboard? Get it examined.
