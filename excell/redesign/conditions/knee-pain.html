Where it hurts

Knee pain

Running, squatting, stairs, or just getting out of the car. Find out why your knee hurts.

Book My First Visit

Why see an extremity specialist

Dr. McCray is a Certified Chiropractic Extremity Practitioner, trained in the joints beyond the spine, including the knee, hip, ankle and foot. Knees are part of his everyday work with athletes and active people.

Common reasons knees hurt

  • Pain around or behind the kneecap ("runner's knee"): often worse on stairs, squats or after sitting a long time.
  • Pain on the outside of the knee: often linked to the IT band in runners and cyclists.
  • Pain just below the kneecap ("jumper's knee"): common in jumping and running sports.
  • Stiff, achy knees that are worse in the morning or after activity.
  • Problems that start above or below: how your hips and feet move changes the load on your knees.

How we figure out yours

Your exam checks the knee itself, plus your hips, ankles and feet, and how you walk, squat and balance. X-rays are taken only if there's a clinical reason.

What care can include

  • Adjustments of the knee, hip, ankle or foot when the exam calls for it
  • Myofascial release or cupping for tight thigh and calf muscles
  • Exercise rehab to strengthen the hips and legs that support the knee
  • Custom orthotics (Foot Levelers) if your feet are part of the problem

When we refer you out

If the exam points to a possible torn ligament or meniscus, a fracture, or something else that needs imaging or a surgeon, Dr. McCray tells you and refers you to the right provider.

Go to urgent care or the ER if

  • You can't put weight on the leg after an injury
  • The knee swelled up fast, locks, or gives way
  • It's hot, red and swollen, especially with a fever, or your calf is swollen and painful
