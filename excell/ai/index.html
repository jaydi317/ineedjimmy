The one rule: the patient line

Nothing about patients goes into any AI app. Neither Muse nor your ChatGPT plan has a HIPAA agreement. That's the paperwork that would make patient information legal to share. So treat these assistants like a smart friend who doesn't work at the office.

Never connect or type
  • ChiroTouch, or anything with patient records
  • The office email or texts where patients write in
  • Patient names, health details, insurance or birthdays
  • Your personal email that gets bank or security codes
  • Passwords or card numbers, typed into the chat
Fair game
  • Marketing ideas, video scripts, website wording
  • Your Instagram and Facebook business page
  • Research: competitors, equipment, CE courses
  • The shared Notion page you and I use
  • Personal life: trips, gifts, dinner plans, your week

When in doubt, ask yourself: "Would I be OK if this showed up on a billboard?" If not, keep it out. Or text me first.

Set up Muse in 10 minutes

Do this on your iPhone. Skip connecting apps for now; just talk to it.

  1. Get the app

    Open the App Store and search Muse from Meta (made by Meta Platforms, free). It needs iOS 18 or newer. Or open muse.ai in Safari or on the office Mac.

  2. Sign in

    Use your Facebook or Instagram login, or make a free account. It will ask you to confirm who you are and your age. That's normal.

  3. Name it

    Give it a name you like saying. Mine is Jarvis. It will offer to connect apps. Tap skip for now.

  4. Flip two switches

    In Settings → Data controls, turn off AI training (it starts on). Wherever it asks about approvals, pick Always ask. Never "Always allow."

  5. Talk, don't type

    Tap the microphone and talk like you'd talk to me. Long and rambly is fine; it sorts it out.

  6. Paste the first message below

    "Get to know me" lets it interview you about the business, one question at a time, so it starts out knowing you.

  7. Leave apps for Monday

    We'll connect only the safe ones together, and we'll set each one to read-only unless it truly needs more.

Your first week: try these

Tap Copy, then paste into Muse. These work in your $20 ChatGPT too. Do one a day; no homework pressure.

Day 1 · Get to know me

I'm a sports chiropractor who owns a practice in Indianapolis. Interview me one question at a time so you understand my business, my week, and what drains my time. Business only — never ask about patients. After about 10 questions, tell me the 3 things you could take off my plate first.

Day 2 · What can you do?

What can you do for me, and which apps can you connect to? I run a healthcare office, so I can't share patient information with you. Tell me which connections are safe for me and which to avoid.

Day 3 · Video ideas

Give me 10 short video ideas (under 60 seconds) a sports chiropractor could film on a phone. Each one should answer a question patients really ask, like "Is decompression safe?" or "Why does my back hurt after sitting all day?" Keep the language simple and friendly.

Day 4 · Website "likes"

I'm redoing my practice website. Ask me about 5 quick yes-or-no questions at a time to figure out what I want: the look, what goes on the home page, videos, booking, and patient resources. When we're done, give me a short list I can text to my web guy, Jimmy.

Day 5 · Just for fun

Plan a relaxed Saturday for me and my family in Indianapolis this weekend: one thing outside, one good place to eat, and one backup if it rains. Keep it under $150.

Any time · Ideas inbox

Remember this idea for my practice so we can come back to it: [say your idea]. Keep a running list of my ideas and read it back to me when I ask "what are my ideas?"

How it keeps you safe

  • It asks first, if you keep it that way. With approvals on "Always ask," Muse won't send, post or buy anything without your OK. If it asks and you're not sure, say no.
  • You can see everything it did. It keeps a running log of what it's done and what it plans to do.
  • It never sees your passwords. When you connect an app, you log in on that app's own screen.
  • You can take it back. Disconnect any app in Settings → Connectors, or tell it "forget that."
  • It can be wrong. Treat it like a sharp new hire: great first drafts, but you check before anything goes out.

Why you don't see "Dot"

Dot is ChatGPT's new always-on assistant. It launched at the end of September. It works in the background, follows up on things, and you can even call it.

It's included with ChatGPT Pro (starts at $100 a month) and Business Premium for teams, not the $20 Plus plan you have. OpenAI says it's coming to "more users soon," but hasn't said when or whether Plus is included. That's why I see it and you don't.

My advice: don't upgrade for it right now. Muse does most of the same everyday things for free, and your $20 ChatGPT is still great for writing, questions and talking things through. If Muse becomes part of your day and you want more, we'll look at Dot together.
