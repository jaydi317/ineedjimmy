05 · Phones and front desk

AI that answers, your desk that stays

You said the dream is a phone system that turns a caller into a booked appointment, any time of night, while patients still get a real person when it matters. Here's what we found.

What's true today

  • ChiroTouch has no open connection for outside apps to book straight into your calendar. Anyone promising that outside ChiroTouch's own partner list is guessing.
  • Grasshopper won't sign a HIPAA agreement (a BAA), so it shouldn't handle patient details by text.
  • Deadline: ChiroTouch's built-in card processing (OpenEdge / Global Payments) ends Dec 30, 2026. CT Pay replaces it. Worth checking if this affects you.

What we recommend

  1. One call with your ChiroTouch rep to turn on what you may already pay for: reminders, two-way texting, recall, and post-visit review requests.
  2. An AI receptionist that answers 24/7, answers the common questions, texts your ChiroTouch booking link, and hands off to your front desk. Leading option: Quo with Sona AI (about $80–130 a month). Backup: RingCentral Advanced.
  3. One-week test before anything changes for patients.

Also worth a demo only: Aloha with Charlie AI, a ChiroTouch partner that claims booking inside ChiroTouch. We'd only sign up if a test call puts a real appointment on your calendar. Prices are as published in October 2026 and should be confirmed.

06 · Reviews, no-shows and past patients

Quick wins that cost nothing

Reviews

Bring back the automatic thank-you email with a review link, like your old system. Fresh reviews matter more than the star rating. The goal is 4–8 new ones a month.

No-shows

Your 7 a week are random and most reschedule. A same-day "we missed you, here's your link" text catches them before they drift.

Past patients

A short note in your voice, no discount, one booking link. Start with 40 people. 6–12 months: a text. 12–24 months: a text plus a call for former members. Over 24 months: one text, then stop.

07 · Referrals

Thank the people who already send you patients

Existing patients send you the most people, and you haven't done thank-yous in over 10 years. The simplest restart: a handwritten thank-you to everyone who referred someone, plus an easy "send this to a friend" link and video. The retired NFL players already know each other through the alumni chapter, so a page just for them could travel fast.

Indiana rule

Indiana doesn't allow paying for referrals (846 IAC 1-3-3). No gift cards, credits or free months for referring. A sincere thank-you note is fine, and so is a new-patient offer for the person who was referred. Have your attorney confirm anything new before it goes out.

Inside your plans an adjustment works out to about $53. Chains sell volume at about $20 a visit; sports clinics sit at $75–90. You're not underpriced compared with a chain, and there's room to add value rather than cut price.

What works

  • Offer it at the end of active care, not on visit one
  • 2–3 plans, simple enough for the front desk
  • Visits that reset monthly (no guilt "bank")
  • One written freeze a year (football season, travel)
  • A menu of treatments, not only adjustments

What flops

  • Cheap "unlimited": fills your book, drops your income per hour
  • $30-a-year discount clubs
  • Big prepaid plans (you've already seen those fail)
  • Cancel mazes and long lock-ins
  • Paying for referrals (not allowed in Indiana)

25 charter spots on Same Hands, offered by invitation when a patient finishes their first round of care. The price stays locked as long as they stay. Month to month, text to cancel, no contract. What you'd say, in about 30 seconds:

"You're moving well again. Most people stop here and come back in a year when it hurts. My best patients stay on a simple monthly plan: same table, same hands, twice a month, plus the recovery tools. No contract. I'm opening 25 charter spots at a price that stays locked as long as you're with us. Want one?"

Before selling it

  • A short written agreement (what's included, how to cancel, "this is not insurance") reviewed by an Indiana healthcare attorney.
  • Keep memberships cash-only and off insurance claims. Check your Cigna contract before enrolling Cigna patients.
  • Recovery plans need the rehab and massage hire to run the sessions.
  • Prices here are research-based starting points. Nothing is final until you tell us what would make a membership feel right, and what would make it feel wrong.
